About AFAB Nation

AFAB Nation is a female-centered community created for women who believe that being a woman is rooted in sex, not identity, pronouns, clothing, stereotypes, or performance.

We watched the definition of woman become one of the most contested definitions in modern culture.

We watched women who questioned that change get called hateful, intolerant, exclusionary, or TERFs.

We watched arguments over women's sports, bathrooms, locker rooms, shelters, prisons, healthcare, language, statistics, and other sex-based spaces become arguments women were increasingly expected to stay quiet about.

AFAB Nation exists because we are not staying quiet.

Who We Are

We are AFAB: Assigned Female at Birth.

In Spanish, we have our own version:

AMAN: Asignada Mujer Al Nacer.

And yes, some of us proudly reclaim another label:

TERF: Totally Excellent Real Female.

We are women with different politics, religions, sexual orientations, personalities, backgrounds and opinions.

We don't agree about everything.

We don't need to.

What brings this community together is our conviction that female people have the right to organize, speak and advocate around our sex.

Womanhood Is Not a Costume

Our message isn't that women must look or behave a certain way.

It's almost the opposite.

Womanhood is not a pronoun.
Womanhood is not a feeling.
Womanhood is NOT cosplay.

A woman doesn't become more female by wearing lipstick, heels or a dress.

A woman doesn't become less female because she has short hair, hates makeup, works construction, loves motorcycles, dates women, or refuses every feminine stereotype ever invented.

Women spent generations fighting the idea that dresses, mannerisms and social roles define us.

We're not bringing those stereotypes back and calling them progressive.

Taking Back Women's Spaces

This is the heart of AFAB Nation.

We believe women have the right to advocate for female-only spaces and categories where sex matters, including conversations about sports, privacy, changing facilities, shelters, healthcare and other settings involving sex-based needs.

Women saying “this space is for us” should not automatically be treated as hatred toward somebody else.

A boundary is a boundary.

And women are allowed to have them.

What About Trans Women?

AFAB Nation openly disagrees with a central claim common in transgender activism: we do not believe identifying as a woman makes someone female.

That disagreement is fundamental to this community.

But disagreement about sex and women's spaces is not permission to threaten, stalk, doxx, harass or abuse individual transgender people. We can criticize ideas, policies, institutions and political movements without treating individual human beings as targets.

Likewise, women here will not be required to affirm beliefs about sex or womanhood that they do not hold as a condition of participating.

Respect does not require agreement.

Why AFAB Nation Exists

We're building the place we wanted to see:

  • A social community.
  • A media platform.
  • A music platform.
  • A place for women's commentary.
  • A place for memes and satire.
  • A place where women can disagree.
  • A place where women can advocate for sex-based rights and spaces.

And, because apparently civilization has become strange enough to require this sentence:

A place where women are allowed to talk about being women.

We intend to do it with arguments, receipts, music, memes, humor and one hell of a soundtrack.

XX. AFAB. AMAN. TERF.

Female is not a stereotype.

Womanhood is not a performance.

Women's boundaries are not hatred.
